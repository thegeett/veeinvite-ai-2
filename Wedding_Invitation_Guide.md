# Part 1 — Universal Invitation Content

## What Every Wedding Invitation Must Include

These elements are required across virtually all cultures and wedding styles. They form the core of the main invitation card.

---

### 1. The Host Line

Names the people hosting (paying for) the wedding. Traditionally the couple's parents; modern couples increasingly host themselves.

- **Traditional:** "Mr. & Mrs. James Hartley request the honor of your presence..."
- **Both families:** "Together with their families, Emma & Liam invite you..."
- **Couple-hosted:** "Emma Rose Hartley & Liam James Chen invite you..."
- **If a parent is deceased:** "Daughter of the late Mr. & Mrs. Thomas Reid"

---

### 2. The Request Line

Formal phrase that sets the tone of the invitation. Different phrases signal different formality levels.

- **Most formal (religious venue):** "Request the honor of your presence"
- **Formal (secular venue):** "Request the pleasure of your company"
- **Semi-formal:** "Invite you to celebrate the marriage of"
- **Casual:** "Would love for you to join them as they wed"

---

### 3. Couple's Names

- Most prominent text on the invitation — often in the largest typeface
- Formal: full legal names including middle names
- Casual: first names only or nicknames
- Traditional heterosexual: bride's name first; modern couples choose any order
- Same-sex couples: alphabetical or personal preference

---

### 4. Date & Time

- Spell out the full date — day of week, month, day, year
- Formal style: "Saturday, the fourteenth of June, two thousand twenty-five"
- Modern style: "Saturday, June 14, 2025"
- Time: spell out formally — "at half past four in the afternoon" or "4:30 PM"
- Include ceremony start time (not arrival time)

---

### 5. Ceremony Venue & Address

- Name of venue or place of worship
- Full street address, city, state/province
- Zip/postal code for out-of-town guests
- For destination or remote venues: link to map or directions insert

---

### 6. Reception Information

- If same venue: "Reception to follow" or "Dinner and dancing to follow"
- If different venue: include on a separate reception card (see Part 2)
- Time if there is a gap between ceremony and reception

---

### 7. RSVP Details

- Deadline: typically 3–4 weeks before the wedding date
- Method: reply card with pre-stamped envelope, online form, or email
- URL to digital RSVP is increasingly common
- Space for dietary restrictions or meal selection (plated dinners)

---

### 8. Dress Code

| Code | Expectation |
|---|---|
| Black Tie | Tuxedo / floor-length gown |
| Black Tie Optional | Tuxedo optional; dark suit / formal gown |
| Formal / White Tie | White tie and tails / ball gown |
| Cocktail / Semi-Formal | Suit or cocktail dress |
| Smart Casual | Blazer / sundress |
| Casual | No strict requirement |
| Themed | Garden party, beach formal, festive attire |

---

### 9. Wedding Website *(Strongly Recommended)*

- Short URL that guests can visit for more detail
- Contains: registry, accommodations, FAQ, maps, event schedule
- Better etiquette to link website than to list registry directly on invite

---

## What Is Commonly Added (Optional but Standard)

### Additional Details

- Parking instructions (especially for urban or complex venues)
- Transportation / shuttle info
- Accommodations hotel block
- "Adults only" note — best communicated via website, not on the invite
- Children welcome — sometimes explicitly stated
- "No gifts please" or "In lieu of gifts, donations to [charity]"
- "Unplugged ceremony" request — asking guests to put away devices
- "No perfume/cologne" for fragrance-sensitive guests

---

### What Should NOT Go on an Invitation

- Registry information directly on the main card — use website instead
- Explicit "adults only" language — address envelopes to adults only
- Gift expectations (monetary or otherwise) — exceptions exist culturally
- Excessive personal text that clutters the essential information
- Negative language ("Do not bring children")

# Part 2 — All Card Types in the Suite

> **Suite vs. Stationery:** The *invitation suite* refers only to cards sent inside the same envelope as the main invitation. The *wedding stationery suite* is broader and includes save-the-dates, programs, thank-you notes, and day-of paper goods.

---

## Card 1 — Main Invitation Card

**The centerpiece. Announces the wedding with all core details.**

**Standard Size:** 5" × 7" (A7)

**Must include:**
- Host line
- Request line
- Couple's full names
- Date and time (spelled out for formal, date format for casual)
- Ceremony venue name and address
- Reception line ("to follow" or reference to separate reception card)
- Dress code *(if not on details card)*
- Wedding website URL *(if not on details card)*

> 💡 *Tip: Keep all essential details on the front. Details on the back risk being missed.*

---

## Card 2 — RSVP / Response Card

**How guests confirm attendance. One of the most functionally important cards.**

**Standard Size:** 4.75" × 3.5" (A1) with pre-stamped return envelope

**Must include:**
- Reply-by deadline date
- Line for guest name(s)
- Attendance checkbox: "Accepts with pleasure" / "Regretfully declines"
- Number of guests attending *(if plus-ones are offered)*
- Meal choice *(for plated dinners: typically 2–3 options)*
- Dietary restrictions or food allergies field
- Optional extras: song request for DJ, message to the couple

> 💡 *Modern alternative: Online RSVP via wedding website — still include a physical card for older guests.*
>
> 💡 *Pro tip: Number RSVP cards on the back to match your guest list — helps decode illegible handwriting.*

---

## Card 3 — Reception Card

**Required when the reception is at a different location from the ceremony.**

**Standard Size:** 4.25" × 5.5" (A2) or smaller insert

**Must include:**
- Reception venue name
- Full address
- Start time *(if not immediately after ceremony)*
- "Cocktail hour begins at X / Dinner at Y"
- Parking notes
- Shuttle or transport info
- Dress code *(if different for reception)*

---

## Card 4 — Details / Information Card

**A catch-all insert for information that does not fit on the main card.**

**Standard Size:** 4.25" × 5.5" or custom

**May include:**
- Wedding website URL
- Dress code *(if not on main card)*
- Parking instructions
- Shuttle schedule
- Event timeline overview
- "Unplugged ceremony" note
- Special ceremony notes (e.g., outdoor venue, standing ceremony, religious customs for guests)

> 💡 *Many couples combine the details card and the accommodations card to save on printing.*

---

## Card 5 — Accommodations Card

**Essential for destination weddings or guests traveling from out of town.**

**Standard Size:** Small insert or combined with details card

**Must include:**
- Name of hotel(s) with room block reservations
- Booking code or group name to use
- Deadline to book at the group rate
- Distance from ceremony / reception venues
- 2–3 alternative accommodation options at different price points
- Airport information for destination weddings

---

## Card 6 — Map / Directions Card

**Particularly useful for remote venues, multi-venue events, or destination weddings.**

**Standard Size:** Same as or slightly larger than main card

**May include:**
- Custom illustrated map showing ceremony and reception locations
- Driving directions from major highway or landmark
- Parking lots or instructions
- QR code linking to Google Maps
- Notes about GPS accuracy issues *(important for rural venues)*

---

## Card 7 — Weekend Itinerary Card

**For multi-day or destination weddings with multiple hosted events.**

**Standard Size:** Larger card or folded booklet

**May include:**
- Welcome dinner (Thursday/Friday night) — venue, time, RSVP
- Wedding ceremony and reception details
- Post-wedding brunch — venue, time, RSVP
- Optional activities (spa, golf, tours) with booking info
- Dress code for each event
- Contact info for questions

---

## Card 8 — Registry Card

**Controversial in traditional Western etiquette; increasingly common as a small insert.**

**Standard Size:** Business card size (2" × 3.5")

**May include:**
- Store names and website URLs
- OR simply: "Registry information available at [wedding website URL]"

> ⚠️ *Traditional etiquette: Never include registry on main card. Use website or small insert.*

---

## Card 9 — Save the Date *(Pre-Suite)*

**Sent months before the formal invitation. NOT part of the invitation suite, but the first communication.**

**Timing:** 6–8 months before (local); 8–12 months before (destination)

**Must include:**
- Couple's names
- Wedding date
- City and state *(full venue details come with formal invite)*
- "Formal invitation to follow"
- Wedding website URL

---

## Card 10 — Envelope Suite

**The envelope is not just packaging — it's part of the first impression.**

### Outer Envelope
- Guest's full name, title, and mailing address (handwritten or printed)
- Return address on back flap
- Postage (ideally a special stamp)
- Wax seal or sticker seal

### Inner Envelope *(Traditional / Formal)*
- Guest's name only — no address
- Indicates exactly who is invited (e.g., no inner envelope = no plus-one)
- "Mr. & Mrs. Thompson" or "Sarah & Guest"

### RSVP Return Envelope
- Pre-addressed with couple's address
- Pre-stamped as a courtesy
- Matches design aesthetic of the suite

---

## Day-of Paper Goods *(Not in the Suite)*

These are ordered separately but should match the suite's visual identity.

| Item | Item |
|---|---|
| Ceremony Program | Seating Chart Display |
| Menu Cards | Favor Tags |
| Escort / Place Cards | Vow Booklets |
| Table Numbers | Photo Backdrop Signage |
| Welcome Signs | Buffet Labels |
| Bar Menu | Thank-You Cards |

# Part 3 — Cultural Wedding Invitation Guide

> Wedding invitations differ fundamentally by culture. What counts as essential content, whose names appear, what symbols are used, and even what additional cards are included all shift depending on cultural and religious background.

---

## 🇺🇸 Western (American / European)

**Core Philosophy:** Couple-centered. The invitation announces an individual union. Hosts are typically parents or the couple themselves.

### Content on Main Card
- Host line (parents or couple)
- Request line (sets formality)
- Couple's names — most prominent
- Date, time, venue address
- Reception info or line
- Dress code
- Wedding website URL

### Typical Card Suite
- Main invitation card
- RSVP card + return envelope
- Details/information card
- Accommodations card *(if needed)*
- Map card *(if needed)*

### Etiquette Notes
- Registry: website only — never on the main card
- Envelope addressing: inner envelope specifies exactly who is invited
- "Adults only" is communicated by addressing envelopes to adults, not explicit text
- Send 6–8 weeks before (local) or 3 months before (destination)

---

## 🇮🇳 South Asian — Hindu, Sikh, Muslim

**Core Philosophy:** Family-centered. The wedding is a union of two families, not just two individuals. Events span multiple days with separate ceremonies, each requiring its own invitation.

### Who Gets Prominent Billing
- Parents' full names lead the invitation — not the couple
- Grandparents may also be listed as co-hosts
- Both maternal and paternal family lines named

### Religious Opening
- **Hindu:** Shree Ganeshaya Namah (invoking Ganesha) — often illustrated
- **Sikh:** Ik Onkar symbol and Waheguru blessing
- **Muslim:** Bismillah-ir-Rahman-ir-Rahim (In the name of Allah)
- Sanskrit shlokas or religious verses for Hindu invitations

### Multi-Event Cards *(each event may have its own card)*
- 🌿 **Mehendi card** — henna ceremony (female-centric, smaller gathering)
- 💛 **Haldi card** — turmeric ceremony (yellow-themed, pre-wedding ritual)
- 🎶 **Sangeet card** — music and dance celebration (all guests)
- 🐎 **Baraat note** — groom's procession details for ceremony day
- 🪔 **Main Wedding Ceremony card** — the Saat Pheras / Anand Karaj / Nikah
- 🎉 **Reception card** — celebration dinner

### Design Language
- **Colors:** Red (auspicious, main), Gold, Maroon, Deep purple
- **Motifs:** Ganesha, lotus (purity), peacock (beauty/joy), elephant (good fortune)
- Paisley and mandala patterns throughout
- Marigolds and jasmine flower elements
- Heavy use of foil embossing, velvet covers, and premium papers

### Dress Code Note
Explicitly state dress code for each event — traditional attire (saree, lehenga, sherwani, kurta) is expected at ceremony; cocktail/fusion is fine at reception.

### RSVP
Traditional Indian invitations historically did not include RSVPs — attendance was assumed. Modern invitations now commonly include them, especially for diaspora weddings.

---

## 🇨🇳 Chinese — Mainland, Cantonese, Taiwanese, Diaspora

**Core Philosophy:** Family-centered. The banquet is as important as the ceremony. Gifts come as cash in red envelopes. Parents traditionally send invitations on behalf of the couple.

### Opening Symbols
- Double happiness character (囍) — nearly always present at the top
- Dragon & Phoenix imagery — dragon = groom's strength, phoenix = bride's grace
- Mandarin ducks — symbolize lifelong love and fidelity
- Peonies, cherry blossoms, plum blossoms

### Color
- **Red envelope** — luck, joy, happiness, and celebration
- **Gold accents** — wealth and divine blessing
- Red and gold is the dominant palette

### Content Unique to Chinese Invitations
- Both parents' full names listed (key mark of respect)
- Both lunar calendar date AND Gregorian date — especially for auspicious alignment
- Wedding banquet time and venue details (banquet = focal event)
- Bilingual (Chinese and English) for diaspora families

### Additional Cards in the Suite
- 🍵 **Tea Ceremony Card** — invites close family to serve and receive tea; sent to parents, grandparents, aunts/uncles only
- 🎂 **Wedding Cake Card** *(Bride's family)* — redeemable card for wedding pastries distributed to bride's guests
- 🧧 **Red Packet** *(Groom's family)* — small cash envelope sent with invitations as advance gesture of courtesy to guests

### Etiquette Notes
- No save-the-dates in traditional Chinese custom — send invitations 3 months before
- Deliver invitations to elders in person — shows deep respect
- Address envelopes with honorifics (e.g., Dear Uncle: 伯父大人)
- Specify if whole family or couple only is invited using Chinese honorifics

---

## ☪️ Muslim / Islamic

**Core Philosophy:** Faith-centered. The Nikah (marriage contract) is the spiritual heart of the wedding. Invitations reflect Islamic values: modesty, family lineage, and divine blessing.

### Religious Opening
- **Bismillah-ir-Rahman-ir-Rahim** — "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful"
- Written in Arabic calligraphy and transliteration
- Quranic verse — most common: Al-Rum 30:21 on love and mercy
- Islamic dua (prayer): "Barakallahu Lakuma" — May Allah bless you both

### Unique Content
- Nikah ceremony named explicitly (Islamic marriage contract)
- Walima (reception) listed as a separate event
- Full names of bride and groom with "Son/Daughter of" to honor lineage
- Names of grandparents sometimes included
- Islamic (Hijri) date alongside Gregorian date
- Both sets of parents listed as hosts

### Additional Cards
- Mehndi / Henna card *(South Asian Muslim)*
- Walima card *(separate reception after Nikah)*
- Separate invitations for gender-segregated events if applicable

### Design Notes
- Arabic calligraphy is a centerpiece design element
- Geometric Islamic patterns (epitomize balance and harmony)
- No human figures on traditional invitations
- **Colors:** Green (Islamic), Gold, White, Deep blue
- Persian roses, Mughal lotus, crescent moon motifs

### Etiquette
- Avoid images of the bride and groom on the card
- No alcohol-related language or imagery
- Quranic verses must be handled respectfully — not discarded carelessly

---

## ✡️ Jewish

**Core Philosophy:** Joy-centered and family-honoring. The tone emphasizes celebration (Simcha) rather than formality. Both families' names are listed as a sign of unified joy.

### Opening & Tone
- Traditional opening: Hebrew initials **ב׳ס׳ד׳** (Bet-Samech-Dalet) in upper-right corner — signifying divine approval
- Tone: "With joy in our hearts" — not "Request the honor of your presence"
- "Share in our Simcha" or "Join us as we celebrate" common phrases
- "X and Y" with the word "and" — not "marriage of X to Y"

### Unique Content
- Hebrew date alongside Gregorian date — use Jewish calendar to find corresponding date
- Both sides: Left column Hebrew / Right column English *(common in Orthodox communities)*
- Hebrew names used *(especially for Sephardic, Mizrahi, or Israeli relatives)*
- **Chuppah time** listed separately from cocktail hour — guests must not miss this moment
- **Bedeken ceremony** time *(if applicable)* — where groom veils the bride
- Both sets of parents named as co-hosts

### Additional Cards
- **Kabbalat Panim card** — pre-ceremony cocktail hour / reception for the couple separately
- Ketubah signing info for close family
- Shabbat dinner invitation if wedding is Fri/Sat

### Design Notes
- Deep blue and gold most traditional palette
- Star of David, Tree of Life, Jerusalem imagery
- Ornate Hebrew calligraphy
- Some couples include a small Ketubah-inspired border

### Etiquette
Some observant couples omit God's name in Hebrew from invitations — as a sacred text, it cannot be thrown away. They use abbreviations or omit entirely.

---

## 🇳🇬 Nigerian / West African

**Core Philosophy:** Community-centered. Weddings are large communal celebrations. Multiple events across different days, each important. Invitation suites are often the most complex of any culture.

### Unique Content
- Extended family names may appear — not just parents
- Both traditional and church/civil ceremonies listed as separate events
- **Aso-ebi details** — matching fabric worn by invited guests; color, fabric type, and where to purchase may be included as an insert
- Strict event schedules because multiple ceremonies occur

### Event Cards Often Included
- 👑 **Traditional engagement ceremony invitation** (Introduction / Igba Nkwu)
- ⛪ **Church or civil ceremony invitation**
- 🎉 **Reception invitation**
- 👘 **Aso-ebi card** — fabric details and collection point

### Design Notes
- Vibrant, rich colors — purple, gold, royal blue, emerald green
- African print-inspired borders and patterns
- Photos of the couple increasingly common
- Money spray is customary at reception — sometimes noted

---

## 🌮 Latin American — Mexican, Colombian, Brazilian

**Core Philosophy:** Family and community honor. The padrino (godparent/sponsor) system is central — different people sponsor different wedding elements, and they are recognized on the invitation.

### Unique Content
- Father of the bride (and sometimes groom) listed prominently as host
- **Padrinos list** — sponsors named by category:
  - Padrinos de la torta
  - Padrinos del pastel
  - Padrinos de la música
  - Padrinos de los novios
  - Padrinos de las flores
- Catholic Mass details — church name, diocese, priest's name
- Mass time and reception time both clearly listed
- Formal language reflecting family honor

### Cards in the Suite
- Main invitation (often large and ornate)
- Mass / ceremony card
- Reception card
- Padrinos acknowledgment card or list
- RSVP card

### Design Notes
- Elaborate, ornate designs
- Floral motifs — roses, dahlias
- Gold and deep reds / jewel tones
- Bilingual (Spanish and English) for diaspora families
